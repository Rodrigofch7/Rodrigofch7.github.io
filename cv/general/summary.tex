{\small
Applied economist and data scientist working on regulated sectors: who owns water, power and housing, how they are taxed
and governed, and what that does to the households on the other end. Most of my work starts with
building data that did not exist in usable form, including police district boundaries reconstructed
from geocoded incidents, century-old insurance atlases read with a vision-language model, satellite
grids over a new transit corridor, and 1.1 million property tax records joined across six fiscal
years. First author of a peer-reviewed article in \textit{Utilities Policy} on the health effects of
water privatization in Brazil, where the results varied across municipalities and we reported them
that way. I teach the graduate machine learning course at Harris.
